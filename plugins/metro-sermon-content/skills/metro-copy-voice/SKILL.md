# Metro copy voice

Metro copy has two registers. Pick the register from the reader, then apply the shared rules.

## Pick the register

Internal copy is read by the team: tickets, docs, READMEs, plans, status updates, agendas and skill output. Write it spartan, terse and structure first. Target roughly 90 percent ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English): short sentences, one idea per sentence, active voice, imperative verbs for actions, one term for one thing, no filler.

Public copy is read by a visitor, a seeker or a congregant: web pages, welcome lines, video scripts, taglines, blog posts and newsletters. Write it warm and in full sentences. Keep the STE discipline of plain words, active voice and one idea per sentence, but do not clip it. A visitor does not read a spec sheet. Clipped fragments, stacked labels and lists of attributes read as cold. A past rewrite of the Crossroads page drifted to this register and the user corrected it back to a warm, pastoral voice with full sentences and proper ministry names.

When a sermon skill sets its own rules, the skill wins.

## Public copy rules

Make the person who is searching the subject of the sentence. Metro is not the hero. A line like "Nobody searches and comes up empty" beats a line about Metro's presence.

Write one concrete idea per line. Reject word soup: a sentence with several abstract nouns and no picture. Say the thing.

Name ministries and places exactly as the site names them. Use the full ministry name every time on a page, not a mix such as Fellowship, Ministry and Kids. Represent both campuses, East Falls and Cherry Hill, when the copy speaks for the whole church. Check any proper name, such as an event title, against the permanent page for it, and report a spelling or capitalization mismatch.

Invite instead of explaining. Use words a first-time guest knows. Give concrete logistics: where to go, what time, what happens first.

Never name a minor with a full name or grade in public copy. Never copy a block from one campus page to another without checking its facts, such as floors, buildings and entrances.

## Shared rules

No em dashes. Use a comma or a period. En dashes only for number and passage ranges. Straight quotes. Sentence case for headings, except Metro sermon titles, which stay title case. No emojis. Few hyphens. Light use of bold. No bullet points in copy: write sentences, or use numbered steps when order matters. Present tense. Full sentences. No preamble and no heading echo. No aphorisms, no "at its core", no fake candor, no strawmen, no fake options.

## Check before you send

Read the copy once as the reader. Ask whether a first-time visitor understands every word, and whether the copy sounds like a person at the door or a form. Fix the clipped lines first.
