# Metro sermon discussion questions

A good set lets a newer believer or a curious neighbor join the conversation on the first night, and lets the group go deeper when it is ready.

## Source rule

Draw every question from what the pastor actually preached: his points, his images, his illustrations and his gospel turn. Do not add your own theology or illustrations. If you have only a passage and a theme, say so, and keep the questions close to the text.

## Structure

Write three levels, four questions each.

Level 1 is easy entry. These questions have low risk. Anyone can answer from the text or from common experience. They ask what the group noticed.

Level 2 is reflection. These questions ask what the group thinks and how the idea meets ordinary life. They use the sermon's specific images.

Level 3 is personal. These questions ask where the idea touches the person's own trust, fear or habits, and where the gospel answers it. Add one line of framing so a person who is not sure they believe can still answer honestly, for example by answering as someone who is curious. No one is required to share.

If the user supplies their own level definitions, use those.

Open the set with the passage to read together, in NIV 1984.

## Question rules

Write open questions. Avoid yes or no questions, trivia, and leading questions that hide the answer. Avoid church words that a first-time guest would not know. Point each question at a specific image or claim from the sermon. Order the questions so each one builds on the last.

## Facilitation note

End with two or three sentences for the leader. Say how to handle Level 3 with a mixed group, how to protect quiet people, and when to stop and let a silence stand.

## House rules

No em dashes. Straight quotes. Full sentences. Present tense. No bullet points: number the questions within each level.
